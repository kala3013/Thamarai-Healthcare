MALE FERTILITY

Male Factor Infertility

Understanding the causes, diagnosis, and advanced treatments for male factor infertility.

Understanding Male Factor Infertility

Male infertility is one of the major causes of a barren marriage. Male factor contributes to approximately 40-50% of all infertility cases, either as the primary cause or in combination with female factors. At Thamarai Fertility, our andrology specialists provide comprehensive male fertility assessment and treatment.

Did you know? Male factor infertility is equally as common as female factor infertility. Both partners should be evaluated when a couple has trouble conceiving.

Causes of Male Infertility

Male infertility can result from various factors affecting sperm production, sperm function, or sperm delivery:

  • Varicocele: Enlarged veins in the scrotum that affect sperm production
  • Low Sperm Count (Oligospermia): Fewer than 15 million sperm per milliliter of semen
  • Poor Sperm Motility (Asthenospermia): Sperm that do not move properly toward the egg
  • Abnormal Sperm Morphology (Teratospermia): Sperm with abnormal shape or structure
  • Azoospermia: Complete absence of sperm in the ejaculate
  • Genetic Disorders: Y chromosome microdeletions, Karyotyping abnormalities
  • Hormonal Imbalances: Low testosterone, high prolactin, thyroid disorders
  • Obstruction: Blockages in the reproductive tract preventing sperm release
  • Lifestyle Factors: Smoking, alcohol, obesity, stress, environmental toxins

Diagnostic Tests for Male Infertility

Thamarai Fertility offers comprehensive diagnostic services for male infertility:

1. Semen Analysis

The cornerstone of male fertility evaluation. It assesses sperm count, motility, morphology, volume, pH, and other parameters. A complete semen analysis helps identify the specific aspects of sperm health that may be compromised.

2. Y Chromosome Micro Deletion

A genetic test that detects missing segments on the Y chromosome, which can cause severe oligospermia or azoospermia. This is particularly important for men with very low or absent sperm counts.

3. Sperm DNA Fragmentation

Measures the level of DNA damage in sperm. High DNA fragmentation is associated with reduced fertility, lower IVF success rates, and increased miscarriage risk.

4. Karyotyping

A chromosome analysis that identifies numerical or structural chromosomal abnormalities that may affect fertility or be passed to offspring.

Treatment Options

Treatment for male factor infertility depends on the underlying cause and may include:

  • Lifestyle modifications: Improving diet, exercise, and reducing stress
  • Medical management: Hormonal treatments for endocrine disorders
  • Surgical correction: Varicocelectomy for varicocele, vasectomy reversal
  • Intrauterine Insemination (IUI): For mild male factor cases
  • IVF with ICSI: The most effective treatment for severe male factor infertility
  • Sperm Freezing: Preservation of fertility for future use
