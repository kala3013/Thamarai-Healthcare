UNDERSTANDING FERTILITY

Fertility Basics

Understanding the fundamentals of human reproduction - the egg, the sperm, and the miracle of conception.

The Egg and the Sperm: The Beginning of Life

Reproduction is the unique biological function of any organism ensuring continuity of generations and the gene pool. The very beginning of human life starts with two basic cells - the egg from the woman and the sperm from the man.

Did you know? Female age is the single most important factor for fertility. Scientists have shown there is a decline in female fertility after age 35 due to chromosomal aging.

The Female Reproductive System

The egg, also called the oocyte, is a cell which carries the blueprint (chromosomes) of the mother in half. It is housed in the organ called the ovary. Each woman has two ovaries, one on each side of the uterus, close to the opening of the fallopian tubes which is the transit pathway as well as the site of actual union of the egg and the sperm.

The numbers of eggs in each ovary (several million) are determined even before birth. Unlike sperms, the egg numbers do not increase through reproductive life. They only undergo the process of maturation in several stages. The egg is always as old as the woman is, and therefore the age of the chromosomes increases as a woman becomes older.

This implies that fertility treatment should be sought as soon as possible if the woman is past her early 30's.

The Female Reproductive System
Ovaries, fallopian tubes, uterus, and cervix work together in the reproductive process.

Human Egg Cell (Oocyte)
The largest cell in the human body, visible to the naked eye.

The Male Reproductive System

The sperm is a cell which carries the blueprint of chromosomes from the male partner. It is produced in the testis and transported to the outside at the end of the ejaculatory process. The health of the sperm in the semen depends on many factors ranging from genetic makeup to the temperature of the testes.

The three most important features of the semen profile affecting fertility are:

  • Sperm Count - The number of sperm present in the semen
  • Sperm Motility - The ability of sperm to move effectively toward the egg
  • Sperm Morphology - The shape and structure of the sperm

Various internal and external factors can affect the sperm and its transport. The process of erection and ejaculation at intercourse is vital and can be in disorder resulting in infertility. It is important that the male partner is examined and investigated with equal importance.

Male Reproductive Anatomy
The testes produce sperm which travel through the vas deferens during ejaculation.

Human Sperm Cell
A microscopic cell with a head (containing genetic material), midpiece, and tail for propulsion.

The Process of Conception

Conception occurs when a sperm cell successfully fertilizes an egg cell. This typically happens in the fallopian tube. The fertilized egg (zygote) then travels to the uterus where it implants in the uterine lining and begins to develop into an embryo.

For successful conception to occur naturally, several factors must align:

  • Healthy egg must be released from the ovary (ovulation)
  • Healthy sperm must reach and fertilize the egg
  • The fallopian tubes must be open and functioning
  • The uterine lining must be receptive for implantation
  • Proper hormonal balance must be maintained
Important: If you have been trying to conceive for more than a year (or more than 6 months if you are over 35), it is recommended to consult a fertility specialist for evaluation.
